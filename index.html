USU Tuition Proposal Explained

What Utah State University's proposed fall 2027 tuition model would cost each student, where the money goes, and who decides.

01 · The proposal

What's being proposed

For fall 2027, USU proposes one Logan tuition schedule. Differential tuition (extra per-credit charges in some colleges), course fees and most mandatory fees would be folded into tuition, and the flat price for 12–18 credits would be replaced by per-credit pricing: a $900 base charge, ≈$296/credit for credits 1–12, ≈$254 for 13–15 and ≈$192 above 15, plus a $193 building fee. USU says total revenue stays the same, so every dollar one group saves, another group pays.

Who pays less

  • Upper-division business students: today they pay $226.92/credit extra; at 15 credits, 4+ business credits means you save.
  • Statewide residents at most campuses (≈$1,100–1,400/semester less).
  • Graduate students at 9–12 credits (≈$150–225 less for residents; ≈$1,100 less for nonresidents, est.). Graduate loads above 12 credits pay more.
  • Utah State Promise recipients in differential-tuition majors may gain, if Promise covers the folded-in amount (unconfirmed).

Who pays more

  • Most full-time Logan undergrads: +$114 at 12 credits, +$875 at 15, ≈+$1,450 at 18.
  • Engineering, arts, education majors: their current extra charge is too small to offset the increase at 15 credits.
  • Every nonresident at 15 credits, even business majors.
  • Part-time students (+$140–200) and Price/Moab/Blanding students.
  • Anyone on a fixed-dollar scholarship; full-tuition awards absorb most of it.
Bottom line: the proposal moves about $27M in separate college charges into one rate that everyone pays. Business, which funds ≈49% of its budget from differential tuition, is protected; most other Logan students pick up the cost, especially those taking full loads to graduate on time.

Key dates

  • Jan 12–13, 2027: student information sessions (Eccles Conference Center + Zoom)
  • Early March 2027: Truth-in-Tuition public hearing (date not yet posted)
  • Mar 19, 2027: USU Board of Trustees meeting
  • Late March 2027: Utah Board of Higher Education decides (USU says Mar 31; the Board's schedule says Mar 25–26)

How this is calculated
  • Tuition goes into USU's general (E&G) budget, pooled with $278M in state tax funds. Your share is spread across uses in the same proportions as the $451.6M budget (excluding differential-tuition lines): colleges, employee benefits, Statewide campuses, administration, etc.
  • Mandatory fees today ($396.55 Logan) follow USU's 2026–27 fee breakout: building 35.89%, athletics 30.32%, campus rec 14.17%, activity 9.70%, shuttle 6.05%, music & theatre 3.11%, SNAC 0.76%.
  • Differential tuition today goes straight to your college's budget lines for it.
  • Under the proposal: $193 goes to the building fee. Fee-funded services (athletics, rec, shuttle, activities, music & theatre, SNAC) are assumed to keep today's $254/student, since USU says the change is revenue-neutral. About $515 is the per-student share of former college charges (USU's $27.3M of separate charges, minus the ≈$9M of non-building fees, split over 17,743 Logan undergrads, per semester). That pool funds the programs differential tuition and course fees pay for today, split across colleges by their current differential-tuition share. The rest goes into the general budget like tuition does today.
  • These are estimates for illustration; money is pooled, so no individual dollar is literally traceable.

Where the rest of the general budget goes

  • Huntsman depends on differential tuition far more than any other college: about half its budget, versus under 8% elsewhere. USU promises Huntsman keeps $10.8M but “would not receive more money.” The budget book lists $14.9M in Huntsman differential-tuition lines (incl. a $5.5M unassigned holding account); the gap from $10.8M is likely graduate differential tuition, which stays separate, but USU doesn't say.
  • Students are fall 2026 headcount by primary college, all campuses and levels, from USU's enrollment dashboard. Colleges also teach non-majors (Arts & Sciences teaches most general education), so “per student” compares budget to majors, not to who's in the classroom.
  • Huntsman enrollment fell 5% (3,921 → 3,728) between fall 2025 and fall 2026, while Engineering grew 5%. Huntsman's differential tuition works out to ≈$4,000 per business student per year.
  • Differential tuition was frozen for 2026–27. USU told the Board of Higher Education it is “ending the practice of automatically increasing differential tuition with base tuition,” so business and engineering students got smaller increases (1.98% and 2.91%) than everyone else (3.36%).
  • Veterinary Medicine is a separate state line item and is excluded from the new model.
  • USU already moved $3.85M of student fees into tuition this year (“Fees-to-Tuition Initiative”).

How this is calculated
  • Sticker today = full-time base tuition + fees ($9,065/yr resident, $26,510 nonresident; USU 2026–27 schedule) + the college's average differential tuition per student (its 2026–27 differential-tuition budget ÷ its fall 2026 headcount). Averages are low outside business because many students are freshmen or take courses outside their college.
  • Sticker proposed = USU's 15-credit figures × 2 semesters ($10,814 resident, $33,458 nonresident), the same for every college.
  • Aid today = USU's average grant, scholarship, waiver and athletic aid per degree-seeking undergraduate ($120.7M ÷ 19,574 = $6,169; Common Data Set, 2024–25), with the USU-wide average for college-funded scholarships ($389) swapped out for that college's own scholarships per student. Student loans aren't counted as aid.
  • Aid proposed: tuition waivers ($1,598/student today) are assumed to rise with tuition, like full-tuition awards do. Pell, state grants and fixed-dollar scholarships stay the same.
  • Limits: every college gets the same residency mix and USU-wide aid average (real mixes differ); headcounts include graduate and Statewide students; aid is from 2024–25, prices from 2026–27. Arts & Sciences' scholarship total isn't published, so only the former College of Science's $211K is counted, which understates its aid.
  • Cross-check: Huntsman reports 74% of its students get aid at a 72% average discount, ≈53% off sticker overall. This model gives Huntsman ≈37%, so if anything it understates business students' aid, and business students' real net price is lower than shown.
06 · Financial aid

Scholarships

Who really pays differential tuition once scholarships, grants and waivers are counted, how that changes under the proposal, and how much scholarship money each college gives its own students.

Who actually pays differential tuition?

The sticker price isn't what everyone pays. Scholarships, grants and waivers cover part of the bill. One USU rule matters here: “the university's central scholarship budget will be made whole from differential tuition funds for students whose differential tuition is covered by central scholarships or waivers” (CEHHS FY26 report). In plain terms, when a Presidential-type award covers a student's differential tuition, the student's college pays that money back to the scholarship office. So for those students, nobody really pays it: the college goes without.

Students & families (cash or loans)Scholarships, grants & waivers

Huntsman School, FY25: 74% of students received scholarships or grants (not loans), with an average 72% discount off total tuition among recipients (DT Advisory Board minutes, Nov 2025). If aid covers differential tuition in the same proportion as the rest of the bill, students and families pay ≈47% of it and aid covers ≈53%. About 30% of the scholarship support Huntsman students receive comes from the school itself; the rest is USU-wide (admission awards, waivers) and outside sources.

Verified vs. estimated

  • Verified: today's tuition and fees ($4,135.87 + $396.55 = $4,532.42 resident; $12,858.44 + $396.55 = $13,254.99 nonresident, 12–18 credits) against USU's official schedule; all college budgets and differential-tuition lines against the 2026–27 Budget Book; differential tuition rates against USU's 2026–27 table.
  • Verified: fall 2025 and fall 2026 headcount by college (USU enrollment dashboard); undergraduate aid totals (Common Data Set); the “made whole” reimbursement rule (CEHHS FY26 report); Huntsman's 74% / 72% aid figures (Huntsman minutes).
  • From USU's proposal (draft): proposed totals at 12/15/18 credits (resident) and 12/15 (nonresident), and the per-credit rates. Other resident loads are computed from those rates.
  • Estimated: nonresident totals at 13, 14, 16–18 credits (USU's ≈3.17× ratio); graduate rates (110% of the undergraduate formula); the “Your money” allocation (proportional spread of pooled tuition, the ≈$515 per-student share of former college charges, fee-funded services held at today's level); who covers Huntsman's differential tuition (applies the school's overall aid rate); average scholarship sizes.
  • Still not public: total course-fee revenue; how much differential tuition colleges reimburse to central scholarships; how many students hold full-tuition awards by college; the building fee below 12 credits; graduate proposed dollar figures from USU; why Huntsman's budgeted differential tuition ($14.9M) differs from the $10.8M USU cites; what the $750,000 contingency is for; transition-scholarship details.
