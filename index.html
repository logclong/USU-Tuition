USU Tuition Proposal Explained

What Utah State University's proposed fall 2027 tuition model would cost each student, where the money goes, and who decides.

Overview Fact check Your bill By major Budgets Financial aid Take action Sources

What's being proposed

Starting fall 2027, USU wants to charge every Logan student the same price per credit, no matter their major. USU says it would collect the same total. The question is who pays.

What changes

  • The flat price for 12–18 credits ends. You'd pay a base charge plus a price for every credit.
  • Extra charges on some schools' courses end. Their cost moves into everyone's tuition.
  • Most course fees and student fees are folded in, except a separate $193 building fee.

Who ends up paying

Some programs cost more to run. Today, the students who take those courses pay for that. The proposal spreads the cost across everyone, so most students pay more for programs they may never take.

  1. Some programs cost more, and today the students in them pay for it. The business school's extra charge, $226.92 per upper-division credit, funds about half its budget. No other school relies on its charge for more than 8% (2026–27 budget book, USU differential tuition).
  2. The proposal moves that cost onto the majority. About 4 in 5 students in USU's five colleges are outside the business school (3,728 of 20,463, USU enrollment, fall 2026). USU's own FAQ says students whose programs have no extra charge “would likely pay more” (FAQ).
  3. Students with the least room in their budget get no cushion. Pell grants and fixed-amount scholarships don't grow with tuition. 6,025 full-time undergraduates relied on need-based grants in 2024–25 (Common Data Set). USU's promised transition scholarships have no amounts or rules yet.
  4. Finishing on time gets more expensive. Utah law caps most bachelor's degrees at 120 credits, 15 a semester for four years, and pushes for faster degrees (H.B. 265, 2025). Credits 13–18 are free today; under the draft, 15 credits cost $875 more and 18 credits $1,453 more per semester. Today a Logan resident saves about $9,065 by finishing in four years at 15 credits instead of five years at 12. Under the proposal, that saving shrinks to about $3,204.

How aid works with extra charges today

74% of business students got scholarships or grants in 2024–25, averaging 72% off tuition (Huntsman minutes). Business also offers a donor-funded scholarship just for this charge, paying “the lesser of $1,250 per semester or total paid differential tuition” (Hansen scholarship).

“[T]he university's central scholarship budget will be made whole from differential tuition funds for students whose differential tuition is covered by central scholarships or waivers.”

Education, Health & Human Sciences differential tuition report, FY2026

In other words, when a full-tuition award covers a student's extra charge, the college pays that money back, so the college goes without it.

Who decides, and how to weigh in

“This is an early draft, and nothing has been decided. We're sharing it now so you have time to weigh in.”

President Brad Mortensen, email to all students, Nov. 24, 2026
How to weigh in. Come to a student information session on Jan. 12 or 13, speak or submit comments at the Truth-in-Tuition hearing in March, and email the people listed below. USU hasn't posted a separate way to comment on the proposal.
  1. USU President's Office proposes the model (no individual author named). Announced Nov 24, 2026.
  2. Faculty & staff open conversations: Dec 8 and Dec 16, 2026, 11 a.m.–noon, Eccles Conference Center.
  3. Student information sessions: Tue Jan 12, 2027, 10–11 a.m. (Eccles Conference Center) and 5–6 p.m. (Zoom, link “sent later”); Wed Jan 13, 4–5 p.m. (Eccles Conference Center).
  4. Truth-in-Tuition hearing: “early March” 2027; date not posted. Past hearings were in the TSC Ballroom and livestreamed on AggieCast.
  5. USU Board of Trustees votes: meetings Jan 15 and Mar 19, 2027.
  6. Utah Board of Higher Education decides: USU says Mar 31; the Board's schedule lists tuition for Mar 25–26, 2027.

“State law requires a public hearing whenever an institution considers increasing undergraduate tuition, so students and the public can comment before a decision is made.”

Precedent: for 2026–27 USU asked for 3.97% and the Board approved 3.22%. The Board does change USU's numbers.
